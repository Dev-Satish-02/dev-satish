# Art direction: one episode, built from the staircase

**The staircase is the source of truth.** Everything after it is built with the same tools,
in the same rendering language. It is one Three.js renderer configured exactly as the
stairwell is:

- ACES filmic tone mapping, PCF shadows, exponential haze fog and the same bloom (0.28 / 0.5 / 0.88).
- One sun from one side with a soft hemisphere fill.
- Additive light shafts that cross the space in parallel, and drifting dust.
- Matte materials around roughness 0.7, with bronze edges and fittings.
- Windows drawn as canvas textures that glow by emission, the way the stairwell's curtain-wall
  panes do.

Seasons change the haze and the sun, never the method. The fall ends in a flat field of summer
blue, and every later cut works the same way: the scene thickens into its haze, the frame
becomes one flat colour, and the next place clears out of it.

The text uses the hero's card language and nothing else: the paper panel, the monospaced scene
tag with its dash, the display serif, and the black ink box for the role.

## The staircase's rules, as used everywhere

| Element | In the staircase | Carried into every set |
|---|---|---|
| Renderer | ACES filmic, exposure 1.0, PCF shadows | Identical settings, one shared renderer |
| Light | Hemisphere fill and one sun high on one side | The same rig; the sun's colour and angle are seasonal |
| Atmosphere | FogExp2 haze, no floor, sinks into warm haze | Each set has its own haze colour, and cuts happen through haze |
| Materials | Honey-gold steps, bronze nosing and rails, glowing pane texture | Matte surfaces, bronze lines, emissive canvas windows |
| Effects | Soft parallel shafts, dust, light bloom | The same shaft and dust helpers, the same bloom pass |
| Type | Paper card, scene tag, display serif, black role box | Every section opens with the same card |

## References

The places below come from sources that are cited and could be checked. I could not view the
episodes from here, so **no exact timecodes are given**. Each entry names the work, the episode
and the scene, and the real place it was modelled on.

### Summer: Experience, "the line at Iogi"

- **Source.** Japanese Wikipedia, 〈物語〉シリーズ, settings section (citing note 71): the
  live-action images in *Bakemonogatari*'s opening, and the series' railway scenery including
  level crossings (踏切), are drawn from around **Iogi Station on the Seibu Shinjuku Line**, near
  Shaft's studio.
- **The place** (Japanese Wikipedia, 井荻駅):
  - A ground-level station with **two side platforms and three tracks**: a passing line in the
    middle, platforms on the outer two, and a footbridge connecting them.
  - Next to it, the Kanpachi-dōri crossing was one of the road's notorious "crossings that never
    open" (開かずの踏切) until the Iogi Tunnel was built.
- **The train** (Japanese Wikipedia, 西武2000系): the Seibu 2000 series, the line's yellow
  four-door 20 m commuter trains (黄色い電車), which have run mainly on the Shinjuku Line.
- **What is built:**
  - Two side platforms with canopies, three tracks on ballast and sleepers, and catenary portals
    with contact and messenger wires.
  - A footbridge whose window band glows like the stairwell's panes.
  - A level crossing at the platform end, with crossbucks, alternating lamps and barrier arms.
  - Low-rise houses fading into summer haze.
  - A six-car yellow train running left to right as you scroll.
- **The internships** are the station-name signs on the far platform. The camera walks the
  platform from one sign to the next, and the hero-style card beside it gives each one's details.

### Autumn: Projects, "the cram school"

- **Source.** The ruined cram school (学習塾跡) where Oshino stays, first seen in *Bakemonogatari*
  episode 1, *Hitagi Crab*.
  - Japanese Wikipedia says the TV version's cram school evokes **Kowloon Walled City**.
  - For the *Kizumonogatari* films, the cram school is **Kenzo Tange's Yamanashi Bunka Kaikan**
    (Kōfu, 1966). Director Tatsuya Oishi has said the film could exist only because he found
    that building, and Mount Fuji can be seen from it (FUKUKOZY, "なぜ〈物語〉シリーズでは建築が
    描かれるのか"; a Kōfu pilgrimage post confirms the location).
  - Japanese Wikipedia (山梨文化会館) describes the building as floors carried by **cylindrical
    shafts and beams**, with no structural walls inside: Metabolism, built to be extended.
- **What is built:**
  - A grid of tall concrete cylinders with slit windows, deep beams and floor slabs spanning
    between them, some of them missing, as in a ruin.
  - School desks scattered and stacked on the floor where the camera walks.
  - A low six-o'clock sun throwing the cylinders' long shadows, amber haze, and a pale Fuji in the
    distance.
- **Each project** is a case file: a hero-style card with the case number and the result in the
  black role box.

### Winter: Skills & education, "the classroom"

- **Source.** Naoetsu High School. Japanese Wikipedia says it was drawn after a school in
  Mihama-ku, Chiba, and the **Nagoya Congress Center**; a pilgrimage post confirms the latter as
  one cut in the series.
- **The classroom reference** is *Owarimonogatari* episodes 1–2, *Ougi Formula*. Koyomi and Ougi
  are shut in a classroom missing from the school's plans. An on-screen caption dates the event
  to Thursday, July 15th, around 6 p.m. Reviewers note the set is reframed as a cell, a courtroom
  and a vessel, with its colours shifting with the mood.
- **What is built:**
  - A classroom drawn deeper than life, the way Shaft stretches its rooms.
  - Desks and chairs in rows, a full wall of tall windows whose frames throw long shadows across
    the desks, a blackboard and fluorescent fittings.
  - A cold, low winter sun and pale blue-white haze.
- **Skills** are written on the blackboard under the four headings, and repeated in a readable
  card. **Education** is a ruled record card.

### Interlude: Writing, "the library"

- **Source.** *Monogatari Series Second Season*, *Tsubasa Tiger*: Hanekawa goes to the library to
  look up the name Gaen gave the tiger, and finds nothing (TheTVDB episode summary; the fan wiki
  places *Tsubasa Tiger Part 2* on 14 July 2013). Hanekawa is said to have read the town's
  entire library by fifteen.
- **The series' red-and-black type** has a cited origin: the full-screen text cards (テロップ) and
  the red books in Kanbaru's room come from Jean-Luc Godard's films (Japanese Wikipedia).
- **What is built:**
  - A single aisle of floor-to-ceiling shelves receding to one point, filled with thousands of
    individual spines in oxblood, black, bronze and cream.
  - A tall window at the far end with low sun, shafts down the aisle, and lamps glowing in the
    haze.
- **Each post** is one of the books on the shelf. Choosing it in the catalogue card lights that
  spine and draws it forward.

## Cuts between seasons

Each section starts as its haze. As the next section rises:

1. The current set's fog thickens and turns towards the next colour.
2. The frame becomes one flat field, and the section title passes over it like a title card.
3. The next set clears out of the same colour.

The first cut is the staircase's own blue (`#0f47c2`), so the fall lands straight into the
summer sky over Iogi.

## The hero is protected

These are never edited:

- `src/components/StaircaseHero.jsx` and `src/three/staircase.js`.
- The fallback `HeroBlossom.jsx`, with `Petals.jsx`, `lib/particles.js` and `Scene.jsx`.
- The `.stair-*`, `.hero*`, `.scene-tag` and root rules in `index.css`.

The sections reuse the hero's `.scene-tag` markup as it is and copy its card values into their own
classes, so nothing is restyled from outside the hero.
